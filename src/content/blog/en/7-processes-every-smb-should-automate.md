> **Short answer:** The seven processes with the fastest payback are invoice data entry, lead response, meeting notes, spreadsheet cleanup, repetitive customer questions, weekly reporting, and cross-tool data sync. Most run on copy-paste today and can be automated in days, not months. Start with whichever one wastes the most hours this week.

Not every process is worth automating. These seven almost always are — they're repetitive, rule-based, and eat hours that never come back. Here's each one, what it costs you manually, and where to start.

## 1. Invoice and document data entry

Someone reads each invoice and retypes vendor, amount, VAT, and line items into your accounting tool. It's slow, error-prone, and impossible to audit.

A workflow reads the document with a vision model, extracts the fields, validates them, and routes clean data onward — with a human-review path for anything uncertain. In my [invoice-reader demo](/en/projects/), handling drops from about **4 hours a week to roughly 15 minutes**.

## 2. Lead response and follow-up

A lead answered in five minutes converts far more often than one answered in six hours. Yet most inquiries wait — and anything arriving after hours is lost overnight.

Automate scoring and a personalized first reply within minutes, logged to your CRM with a follow-up task. The [lead-response demo](/en/projects/) cuts response time from around **6 hours to under 5 minutes**.

## 3. Meeting notes and action items

Notes are incomplete, action items get lost, and there's no searchable record of what was decided.

Upload the recording: it's transcribed, summarized, and turned into decisions and owner-assigned action items, then distributed. The [meeting-minutes demo](/en/projects/) turns a **one-hour meeting into structured minutes in about two minutes**.

## 4. Spreadsheet cleanup

Critical processes running on fragile spreadsheets mean manual entry, version chaos, and formulas that break silently.

A workflow analyzes the sheet, proposes a clean schema, then deduplicates, standardizes, and validates the data on its way into a proper system. See it on the [spreadsheet-rescue demo](/en/projects/), or read [escaping spreadsheet hell](/en/blog/from-spreadsheets-to-systems/).

## 5. Repetitive customer questions

Your team answers the same handful of questions every day while customers wait.

A retrieval workflow searches your knowledge base and an LLM returns a concise, sourced answer with a confidence score — escalating to a human when it isn't sure. The [support-assistant demo](/en/projects/) answers common questions instantly, around the clock.

## 6. Weekly reporting

If your weekly report means pulling numbers from several systems and formatting them by hand, that's half a day gone every week.

Scheduled aggregation builds the report and lands it in your inbox on time — same format, no manual pull. This is core [document & data workflow](/en/services/document-workflows/) territory.

## 7. Cross-tool data sync

When the same customer record lives in your CRM, your invoicing tool, and a spreadsheet, someone keeps them in sync by hand — and they drift anyway.

Connect the tools so a change in one updates the others in real time, no exports. See [connecting your tools](/en/blog/power-of-integration/) and the [integrations & APIs](/en/services/integrations-apis/) service.

## How to prioritize

Don't automate all seven at once. Pick the one that wastes the most hours this week, fix that, measure the result, then move to the next. [5 signs your business needs automation](/en/blog/5-signs-your-business-needs-automation/) helps you spot which is hurting most.
